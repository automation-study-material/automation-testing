    Specific Volume¶

    1. Definition¶

    Specific volume is the volume occupied by unit mass of a substance.

    In simple words:

    Specific volume tells us how much volume is occupied by 1 kg of fluid.

    It is represented by:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    Where:

    • v = specific volume
    • V = total volume
    • m = mass

    2. Relationship with Density¶

    You already know density:

    \rho = \frac{m}{V}

    Specific volume is the reciprocal of density:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    Therefore:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    and:

    \rho = \frac{1}{v}

    ⭐ Very important AMVI relationship¶

    Density       → mass / volume
Specific volume → volume / mass

They are reciprocals.

    3. SI Unit¶

    Volume:

    m³

    Mass:

    kg

    Therefore:

    v = \frac{m^3}{kg}

    SI unit:¶

    m³/kg

    4. Dimension¶

    We know:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    Volume has dimension:

    [V] = L^3

    Mass has dimension:

    [m] = M

    Therefore:

    [v] = \frac{L^3}{M}

    So:

    Dimension of specific volume = M⁻¹L³

    or:

    L³M⁻¹

    AMVI MCQ¶

    Dimension of specific volume is:

    ✅ M⁻¹L³

    5. Example¶

    Suppose a fluid has:

    Mass = 5 kg
Volume = 2 m³

    Specific volume:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    v = \frac{2}{5}

    v = 0.4 m³/kg

    Answer¶

    Specific volume = 0.4 m³/kg

    6. Example Using Density¶

    Suppose density of a fluid is:

    ρ = 1000 kg/m³

    Then:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    v = \frac{1}{1000}

    v = 0.001 m³/kg

    Therefore, specific volume of water is approximately:

    0.001 m³/kg

    or:

    1 × 10⁻³ m³/kg

    7. Density vs Specific Volume¶

    This is a very important AMVI comparison.

    Property Density Specific Volume
    Symbol ρ v
    Meaning Mass per unit volume Volume per unit mass
    Formula ρ = m/V v = V/m
    Relationship ρ = 1/v v = 1/ρ
    SI unit kg/m³ m³/kg
    Dimension ML⁻³ M⁻¹L³

    🧠 Memory trick¶

    Density = How much mass is packed into a volume.

    Specific volume = How much volume is occupied by a given mass.

    8. Is Specific Volume a Fluid Property?¶

    Yes. Specific volume is an intensive property.

    Intensive property¶

    A property that does not depend on the amount of substance.

    For example:

    • Density
    • Specific volume
    • Temperature
    • Pressure

    Extensive property¶

    Depends on the amount of substance.

    For example:

    • Mass
    • Total volume
    • Total energy

    Example¶

    Suppose you have:

    Tank A¶

    Mass = 10 kg
Volume = 0.01 m³

    Tank B¶

    Mass = 100 kg
Volume = 0.10 m³

    For both:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    Tank A:

    v = \frac{0.01}{10}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg

    Tank B:

    v = \frac{0.10}{100}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg

    Even though the amount of fluid is different, specific volume is the same.

    Therefore:

    Specific volume is an intensive property.

    9. Effect of Temperature on Specific Volume¶

    This is important for AMVI MCQs.

    Remember:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    Therefore:

    Density ↑ → Specific volume ↓

Density ↓ → Specific volume ↑

    Most liquids¶

    For most liquids:

    Temperature ↑
       ↓
Volume ↑
       ↓
Density ↓
       ↓
Specific volume ↑

    Therefore:

    For most liquids, increase in temperature → increase in specific volume.

    Example¶

    Suppose density changes from:

    1000 kg/m³ → 990 kg/m³

    Then initially:

    v_1 = \frac{1}{1000}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg

    After heating:

    v_2 = \frac{1}{990}
\approx 0.0010101\ m^3/kg

    Therefore:

    Temperature ↑ → Density ↓ → Specific volume ↑

    10. Water — Important Exception¶

    Water has an unusual behavior between 0°C and 4°C.

    Water has maximum density at approximately:

    4°C

    Since:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    maximum density means minimum specific volume.

    Therefore:

    At 4°C:
Density → maximum
Specific volume → minimum

    AMVI MCQ¶

    Specific volume of water is minimum at approximately:

    ✅ 4°C

    because water has maximum density at 4°C.

    Water behavior¶

    Temperature: 0°C → 4°C

Density ↑
Specific volume ↓

    Above 4°C:

    Temperature ↑
Density ↓
Specific volume ↑

    Memory¶

    Water: Maximum density = Minimum specific volume at 4°C.

    11. Effect of Pressure on Specific Volume¶

    Again:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    So if pressure changes density, specific volume changes in the opposite direction.

    Liquids¶

    Liquids are only slightly compressible.

    When pressure increases:

    Pressure ↑
     ↓
Density ↑ slightly
     ↓
Specific volume ↓ slightly

    Therefore:

    For real liquids, increasing pressure generally decreases specific volume slightly.

    But in many engineering calculations, liquids are treated as incompressible:

    ρ ≈ constant

    Therefore:

    v ≈ constant

    12. Effect of Pressure on Gases¶

    For an ideal gas:

    PV = mRT

    Divide by mass:

    \frac{PV}{m} = RT

    Since:

    v = \frac{V}{m}

    we get:

    Pv = RT

    Therefore:

    v = \frac{RT}{P}

    This is an extremely important relation for gases.

    At constant temperature¶

    v = \frac{RT}{P}

    Since R and T are constant:

    v \propto \frac{1}{P}

    Therefore:

    For a gas at constant temperature, pressure increases → specific volume decreases.

    If pressure doubles:¶

    P ↑ 2 times
     ↓
Specific volume ↓ to half

    13. Effect of Temperature on Gas¶

    For an ideal gas:

    v = \frac{RT}{P}

    At constant pressure:

    v \propto T

    Therefore:

    For a gas at constant pressure, temperature increases → specific volume increases.

    If absolute temperature doubles:

    T ↑ 2 times
     ↓
v ↑ 2 times

    ⚠️ Important¶

    Gas temperature must be considered in Kelvin when using gas-law proportional relationships.

    T(K) = T(^\circ C) + 273.15

    14. Temperature & Pressure Summary¶

    Fluid / Condition Temperature ↑ Pressure ↑
    Most liquids Specific volume ↑ Specific volume ↓ slightly
    Incompressible liquid v ≈ constant v ≈ constant
    Water, 0–4°C Specific volume ↓ ↓ slightly
    Water, above 4°C Specific volume ↑ ↓ slightly
    Gas, constant pressure Specific volume ↑ —
    Gas, constant temperature — Specific volume ↓

    🔥 AMVI shortcut¶

    Most liquids:
T ↑ → v ↑

Water:
4°C → v minimum

Gas:
T ↑ at constant P → v ↑
P ↑ at constant T → v ↓

    15. Specific Volume and Specific Weight¶

    You have already studied specific weight:

    \gamma = \rho g

    And:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    Therefore:

    \gamma = \frac{g}{v}

    And:

    v = \frac{g}{\gamma}

    Important relationships¶

    \rho = \frac{1}{v}

    \gamma = \rho g

    \gamma = \frac{g}{v}

    These three relationships connect:

    Density ↔ Specific volume ↔ Specific weight

    16. Specific Volume and Pressure Head¶

    Recall:

    h_p = \frac{P}{\rho g}

    Since:

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    we can write:

    h_p = \frac{Pv}{g}

    This is less commonly used than P/(ρg), but it is a useful relationship for understanding the connection between properties.

    17. Specific Volume of Water¶

    At approximately 4°C:

    ρ ≈ 1000 kg/m³

    Therefore:

    v = \frac{1}{1000}

    v ≈ 0.001 m³/kg

    This can also be written as:

    1 × 10⁻³ m³/kg

    or:

    1 litre/kg

    because:

    1 m³ = 1000 litres

    Therefore:

    0.001 m³ = 1 litre

    So approximately:

    1 kg of water occupies 1 litre.

    18. Specific Volume of Air — Concept¶

    Air has much lower density than water.

    Approximately:

    ρair ≈ 1.2 kg/m³

    Therefore:

    v_{air} = \frac{1}{1.2}

    vair ≈ 0.833 m³/kg

    So:

    A small mass of air occupies a comparatively large volume.

    This illustrates the inverse relationship:

    Low density → High specific volume
High density → Low specific volume

    🔥 AMVI MCQ Practice¶

    Q1. Specific volume is:¶

    A. Mass per unit volume B. Weight per unit volume C. Volume per unit mass D. Force per unit area

    Answer: C — Volume per unit mass

    Q2. SI unit of specific volume is:¶

    A. kg/m³ B. m³/kg C. N/m³ D. m²/s

    Answer: B — m³/kg

    Q3. Specific volume is the reciprocal of:¶

    A. Specific weight B. Pressure C. Density D. Specific gravity

    Answer: C — Density

    v = \frac{1}{\rho}

    Q4. If density increases, specific volume:¶

    A. Increases B. Decreases C. Remains same D. Becomes zero

    Answer: B — Decreases

    Q5. Specific volume is:¶

    A. Intensive property B. Extensive property C. Neither D. Dimensionless

    Answer: A — Intensive property

    Q6. For most liquids, increase in temperature causes specific volume to:¶

    A. Increase B. Decrease C. Remain exactly constant D. Become zero

    Answer: A — Increase

    Q7. Specific volume of water is minimum approximately at:¶

    A. 0°C B. 4°C C. 50°C D. 100°C

    Answer: B — 4°C

    Q8. For an ideal gas at constant temperature, pressure increases. Specific volume:¶

    A. Increases B. Decreases C. Remains constant D. Becomes infinite

    Answer: B — Decreases

    Q9. For an ideal gas at constant pressure, absolute temperature doubles. Specific volume:¶

    A. Halves B. Doubles C. Remains same D. Becomes zero

    Answer: B — Doubles

    Q10. Dimension of specific volume is:¶

    A. ML⁻³ B. ML⁻²T⁻² C. M⁻¹L³ D. L²T⁻¹

    Answer: C — M⁻¹L³

    ⚠️ Common AMVI Traps¶

    Trap 1¶

    Specific volume = volume per unit mass, NOT mass per unit volume.

    Specific volume → V/m
Density → m/V

    Trap 2¶

    Specific volume is not dimensionless.

    Its unit is:

    m³/kg

    Trap 3¶

    Density and specific volume move in opposite directions:

    ρ ↑ → v ↓
ρ ↓ → v ↑

    Trap 4¶

    For most liquids:

    T ↑ → ρ ↓ → v ↑

    Trap 5¶

    For water:

    4°C → ρ maximum → v minimum

    Trap 6¶

    For ideal gas:

    Pv = RT

    At constant T:

    P ↑ → v ↓

    At constant P:

    T ↑ → v ↑

    \rho = \frac{1}{v}

    \gamma = \frac{g}{v}

    Pv = RT

    Most important AMVI facts¶

    Specific volume = volume per unit mass

    Unit = m³/kg

    It is the reciprocal of density

    It is an intensive property

    Most liquids: temperature ↑ → specific volume ↑

    Water: specific volume is minimum at approximately 4°C

    Gas at constant temperature: pressure ↑ → specific volume ↓

    Gas at constant pressure: temperature ↑ → specific volume ↑
