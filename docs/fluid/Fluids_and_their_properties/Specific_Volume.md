# Specific Volume

## 1. Definition

**Specific volume** is the **volume occupied by unit mass of a substance**.

In simple words:

> Specific volume tells us how much volume is occupied by **1 kg of fluid**.

It is represented by:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

Where:

* `v` = specific volume
* `V` = total volume
* `m` = mass

---

## 2. Relationship with Density

You already know density:

```math
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
```

Specific volume is the reciprocal of density:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

Therefore:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

and:

```math
\rho = \frac{1}{v}
```

### ⭐ Very important AMVI relationship

```text
Density       → mass / volume
Specific volume → volume / mass

They are reciprocals.
```

---

# 3. SI Unit

Volume:

```text
m³
```

Mass:

```text
kg
```

Therefore:

```math
v = \frac{m^3}{kg}
```

### SI unit:

**m³/kg**

---

# 4. Dimension

We know:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

Volume has dimension:

```math
[V] = L^3
```

Mass has dimension:

```math
[m] = M
```

Therefore:

```math
[v] = \frac{L^3}{M}
```

So:

```text
Dimension of specific volume = M⁻¹L³
```

or:

```text
L³M⁻¹
```

### AMVI MCQ

**Dimension of specific volume is:**

✅ `M⁻¹L³`

---

# 5. Example

Suppose a fluid has:

```text
Mass = 5 kg
Volume = 2 m³
```

Specific volume:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

```math
v = \frac{2}{5}
```

```text
v = 0.4 m³/kg
```

### Answer

**Specific volume = 0.4 m³/kg**

---

# 6. Example Using Density

Suppose density of a fluid is:

```text
ρ = 1000 kg/m³
```

Then:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

```math
v = \frac{1}{1000}
```

```text
v = 0.001 m³/kg
```

Therefore, specific volume of water is approximately:

**0.001 m³/kg**

or:

**1 × 10⁻³ m³/kg**

---

# 7. Density vs Specific Volume

This is a very important AMVI comparison.

| Property     | Density              | Specific Volume      |
| ------------ | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Symbol       | `ρ`                  | `v`                  |
| Meaning      | Mass per unit volume | Volume per unit mass |
| Formula      | `ρ = m/V`            | `v = V/m`            |
| Relationship | `ρ = 1/v`            | `v = 1/ρ`            |
| SI unit      | kg/m³                | m³/kg                |
| Dimension    | `ML⁻³`               | `M⁻¹L³`              |

### 🧠 Memory trick

> **Density = How much mass is packed into a volume.**

> **Specific volume = How much volume is occupied by a given mass.**

---

# 8. Is Specific Volume a Fluid Property?

Yes. Specific volume is an **intensive property**.

### Intensive property

A property that does **not depend on the amount of substance**.

For example:

* Density
* Specific volume
* Temperature
* Pressure

### Extensive property

Depends on the amount of substance.

For example:

* Mass
* Total volume
* Total energy

---

## Example

Suppose you have:

### Tank A

```text
Mass = 10 kg
Volume = 0.01 m³
```

### Tank B

```text
Mass = 100 kg
Volume = 0.10 m³
```

For both:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

Tank A:

```math
v = \frac{0.01}{10}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg
```

Tank B:

```math
v = \frac{0.10}{100}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg
```

Even though the amount of fluid is different, specific volume is the same.

Therefore:

> **Specific volume is an intensive property.**

---

# 9. Effect of Temperature on Specific Volume

This is important for AMVI MCQs.

Remember:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

Therefore:

```text
Density ↑ → Specific volume ↓

Density ↓ → Specific volume ↑
```

---

## Most liquids

For most liquids:

```text
Temperature ↑
       ↓
Volume ↑
       ↓
Density ↓
       ↓
Specific volume ↑
```

Therefore:

> **For most liquids, increase in temperature → increase in specific volume.**

---

## Example

Suppose density changes from:

```text
1000 kg/m³ → 990 kg/m³
```

Then initially:

```math
v_1 = \frac{1}{1000}
= 0.001\ m^3/kg
```

After heating:

```math
v_2 = \frac{1}{990}
\approx 0.0010101\ m^3/kg
```

Therefore:

```text
Temperature ↑ → Density ↓ → Specific volume ↑
```

---

# 10. Water — Important Exception

Water has an unusual behavior between **0°C and 4°C**.

Water has maximum density at approximately:

**4°C**

Since:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

maximum density means **minimum specific volume**.

Therefore:

```text
At 4°C:
Density → maximum
Specific volume → minimum
```

### AMVI MCQ

**Specific volume of water is minimum at approximately:**

✅ **4°C**

because water has maximum density at 4°C.

---

## Water behavior

```text
Temperature: 0°C → 4°C

Density ↑
Specific volume ↓
```

Above 4°C:

```text
Temperature ↑
Density ↓
Specific volume ↑
```

### Memory

> **Water: Maximum density = Minimum specific volume at 4°C.**

---

# 11. Effect of Pressure on Specific Volume

Again:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

So if pressure changes density, specific volume changes in the opposite direction.

---

## Liquids

Liquids are only slightly compressible.

When pressure increases:

```text
Pressure ↑
     ↓
Density ↑ slightly
     ↓
Specific volume ↓ slightly
```

Therefore:

> **For real liquids, increasing pressure generally decreases specific volume slightly.**

But in many engineering calculations, liquids are treated as incompressible:

```text
ρ ≈ constant
```

Therefore:

```text
v ≈ constant
```

---

# 12. Effect of Pressure on Gases

For an ideal gas:

```math
PV = mRT
```

Divide by mass:

```math
\frac{PV}{m} = RT
```

Since:

```math
v = \frac{V}{m}
```

we get:

```math
Pv = RT
```

Therefore:

```math
v = \frac{RT}{P}
```

This is an extremely important relation for gases.

---

## At constant temperature

```math
v = \frac{RT}{P}
```

Since `R` and `T` are constant:

```math
v \propto \frac{1}{P}
```

Therefore:

> **For a gas at constant temperature, pressure increases → specific volume decreases.**

### If pressure doubles:

```text
P ↑ 2 times
     ↓
Specific volume ↓ to half
```

---

# 13. Effect of Temperature on Gas

For an ideal gas:

```math
v = \frac{RT}{P}
```

At constant pressure:

```math
v \propto T
```

Therefore:

> **For a gas at constant pressure, temperature increases → specific volume increases.**

If absolute temperature doubles:

```text
T ↑ 2 times
     ↓
v ↑ 2 times
```

### ⚠️ Important

Gas temperature must be considered in **Kelvin** when using gas-law proportional relationships.

```math
T(K) = T(^\circ C) + 273.15
```

---

# 14. Temperature & Pressure Summary

| Fluid / Condition         | Temperature ↑     | Pressure ↑                 |
| ------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| Most liquids              | Specific volume ↑ | Specific volume ↓ slightly |
| Incompressible liquid     | `v ≈ constant`    | `v ≈ constant`             |
| Water, 0–4°C              | Specific volume ↓ | ↓ slightly                 |
| Water, above 4°C          | Specific volume ↑ | ↓ slightly                 |
| Gas, constant pressure    | Specific volume ↑ | —                          |
| Gas, constant temperature | —                 | Specific volume ↓          |

### 🔥 AMVI shortcut

```text
Most liquids:
T ↑ → v ↑

Water:
4°C → v minimum

Gas:
T ↑ at constant P → v ↑
P ↑ at constant T → v ↓
```

---

# 15. Specific Volume and Specific Weight

You have already studied specific weight:

```math
\gamma = \rho g
```

And:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

Therefore:

```math
\gamma = \frac{g}{v}
```

And:

```math
v = \frac{g}{\gamma}
```

### Important relationships

```math
\rho = \frac{1}{v}
```

```math
\gamma = \rho g
```

```math
\gamma = \frac{g}{v}
```

These three relationships connect:

**Density ↔ Specific volume ↔ Specific weight**

---

# 16. Specific Volume and Pressure Head

Recall:

```math
h_p = \frac{P}{\rho g}
```

Since:

```math
v = \frac{1}{\rho}
```

we can write:

```math
h_p = \frac{Pv}{g}
```

This is less commonly used than `P/(ρg)`, but it is a useful relationship for understanding the connection between properties.

---

# 17. Specific Volume of Water

At approximately 4°C:

```text
ρ ≈ 1000 kg/m³
```

Therefore:

```math
v = \frac{1}{1000}
```

```text
v ≈ 0.001 m³/kg
```

This can also be written as:

```text
1 × 10⁻³ m³/kg
```

or:

```text
1 litre/kg
```

because:

```text
1 m³ = 1000 litres
```

Therefore:

```text
0.001 m³ = 1 litre
```

So approximately:

> **1 kg of water occupies 1 litre.**

---

# 18. Specific Volume of Air — Concept

Air has much lower density than water.

Approximately:

```text
ρair ≈ 1.2 kg/m³
```

Therefore:

```math
v_{air} = \frac{1}{1.2}
```

```text
vair ≈ 0.833 m³/kg
```

So:

> A small mass of air occupies a comparatively large volume.

This illustrates the inverse relationship:

```text
Low density → High specific volume
High density → Low specific volume
```

# ⚠️ Common AMVI Traps

### Trap 1

**Specific volume = volume per unit mass**, NOT mass per unit volume.

```text
Specific volume → V/m
Density → m/V
```

---

### Trap 2

Specific volume is **not dimensionless**.

Its unit is:

```text
m³/kg
```

---

### Trap 3

Density and specific volume move in **opposite directions**:

```text
ρ ↑ → v ↓
ρ ↓ → v ↑
```

---

### Trap 4

For most liquids:

```text
T ↑ → ρ ↓ → v ↑
```

---

### Trap 5

For water:

```text
4°C → ρ maximum → v minimum
```

---

### Trap 6

For ideal gas:

```text
Pv = RT
```

At constant `T`:

```text
P ↑ → v ↓
```

At constant `P`:

```text
T ↑ → v ↑
```
